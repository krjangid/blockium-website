Privacy Policy

How Blockium filters requests, what stays on your device, and when it connects to external services.

Last Updated: September 5, 2026 ZERO TELEMETRY Version 3.1.2

1. Core Philosophy: Absolute Zero Telemetry

Blockium was built on a foundational principle: your browsing activity is private, personal, and belongs exclusively to you.

Blockium performs filtering in your browser and stores dashboard activity on your device. We do not operate external tracking servers, analytics endpoints, or remote profiling databases.

The Blockium Privacy Promise: We do not upload your browsing activity or personal inputs to analytics or profiling servers. Settings and blocking activity used by the dashboard are stored locally on your device, as described below.

2. Information We Do NOT Collect

You do not need an account to use the extension. Its filtering features do not upload personal browsing activity to us:

  • Personal Identifiable Information (PII): No names, email addresses, phone numbers, or account credentials. You can use Blockium immediately without creating an account or logging in.
  • Browsing History & URLs: Filter rules are evaluated inside Chrome. The dashboard keeps local blocked-domain activity as described below; this is not uploaded to us.
  • Form Data & Passwords: Blockium does not collect or transmit passwords, credit card numbers, or form submissions. Content scripts access page elements to carry out filtering features.
  • Analytics & Fingerprinting: We do not use Google Analytics, Mixpanel, Amplitude, or any third-party behavioral analytics scripts inside the extension or on our marketing website.

3. Local On-Device Data Storage

Blockium uses Chrome's local storage API (chrome.storage.local) strictly on your personal device to preserve your preferences across browser sessions:

  • Active Module Toggles: Your customized preferences (e.g., whether Cookie Auto-Dismiss or Threat Alerts are enabled).
  • Custom Filter Rules: Elements you manually remove using the Visual Element Zapper are saved as local CSS selectors on your device.
  • Domain Whitelist: Websites where you have toggled protection OFF or allowed cookie notices.
  • Aggregate Local Statistics: Blocked-request counts, estimated savings, recent activity with blocked domains and timestamps, domain counts, and page-load statistics. These records are stored in local extension storage for the dashboard and are not uploaded.

Your website language choice and dismissed language suggestions are saved in this browser. They are not sent to us.

4. Chrome Permissions & Plain-English Justification

In accordance with Google Chrome Web Store Developer Program Policies, here is the exact technical justification for every permission declared in our manifest.json:

Permission Technical Function & User Benefit
declarativeNetRequest Enables Chrome's native C++ engine to block ad servers and tracking requests before page content renders, without transmitting network traffic to any external server.
declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess Allows declarative network rules to operate on requests covered by the extension’s host permissions.
storage Required to save your user settings (theme preference, whitelisted websites, custom zapper rules) locally on your device.
tabs & webNavigation Used for current-site controls, toolbar counts, navigation events, per-tab statistics, and page-load measurements.
alarms Used to schedule periodic local updates of open-source filter lists (e.g. EasyList) in the background.
notifications Used to display optional local desktop alerts for detected malware or tracker activity.
<all_urls> Necessary for content scriptlets to suppress video ad interruptions (e.g., YouTube), auto-dismiss cookie banners, and apply cosmetic element hiding across all visited domains.

5. Third-Party Services & Network Requests

Automatic filter updates and the website’s support features involve external services:

  • Filter List Updates: The extension periodically downloads updated public, open-source filter rule lists from established repositories (such as https://easylist.to and https://raw.githubusercontent.com). These requests download filter text without attaching browsing history or dashboard statistics. As with other web requests, the provider receives connection information such as your IP address.
  • Website hosting and fonts: The website is served through Cloudflare Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts. Requests to these services include ordinary connection information such as your IP address.
  • Support you choose to send: The report form uses FormSubmit to deliver the information you submit, including your email and report details. Email support also sends your message to our support mailbox. Avoid including passwords or other sensitive information in a report.

6. Data Deletion & User Control

Because all settings and stats exist strictly on your local machine, you have complete and immediate control over your data:

  • Clearing Storage: You can delete custom rules and cookie notice exceptions in the dashboard. Site protection exceptions can be changed in the popup, and the zapper reset restores elements hidden on the current site.
  • Uninstalling: Removing Blockium from Google Chrome immediately and permanently erases all locally stored extension data from your machine.
